Five sims, on your laptop

Every app from the brief, built to run before any hardware exists. Mouse and touch always work; turn on webcam hands in any of them for real skeletal hand tracking through your laptop camera — the same six-gesture grammar the Unity build will use, so what feels right here transfers.

  1. 01
    Orchestra SeatsZip into any of twenty seats and hear the piece from there — HRTF per-seat mix, own instrument loudest. Then play it: percussion by strike, woodwind by fingering + microphone breath, cello pitch-snapped, harp plucked.proves: does the per-seat mix give you chills? (the notes: stop here if not)
  2. 02
    Air DecksTwo loops at 124 and 128 BPM, scratch, pitch faders, crossfader, EQ kill, cue points, a crowd that follows the downbeat when you match.proves: can you beat-match with no haptics? measures audio latency
  3. 03
    Couture WorkshopXPBD cloth on a dress form. Pin and drag, cut with a traced line, gather, smooth, stretch on the bias; silk / jersey / denim presets.proves: do grid-aligned cuts feel like pinking shears, or cheap?
  4. 04
    Air-CADAn L-bracket as a signed-distance field, raymarched. Carve with a traced finger, section it with a palm push, rotate, scale, boolean-subtract with a cancel window.proves: SDF carving at 96³ — where do thin features vanish?
  5. 05
    Air-PaintingSame engine, operator flipped to min, nothing to load. Paint volumes in the air with depth from your hand's distance to the camera.proves: TRACE in 3D — the path every carving gesture depends on

What this is not. Not 6DoF, not world-locked, not a shoulder-mounted ARM chip: a webcam gives 2.5D hands (x, y and a relative depth) at whatever latency your laptop manages — each sim shows that number in its top bar. The two questions the hardware alone can answer are end-to-end latency on the glasses and whether blowing into a neckband microphone feels usable; everything else in the brief is testable here first.
