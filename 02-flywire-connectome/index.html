Connectome Control Room

Four demos — the fly doomscrolling, trading stocks, playing Doom, and playing Beat Saber. All four exist. Three have public repos; Beat Saber is a video demo with no repo, so it gets rebuilt from the same primitives. The firing rates below are measured, not illustrative.

Correction worth having up front
These are MaleCNS, not FlyWire.

MaleCNS — adult male CNS, ~166k neurons and ~125M connections, released early September 2026 by Google Research and HHMI Janelia — is what set off the current wave and what all these demos use. FlyWire is the adult female brain: older, far better annotated, better tooling. Use MaleCNS to make the fly play a game; use FlyWire to understand what you built; use BANC plus NeuroMechFly if you want a real body, because brain-only datasets cannot close the sensorimotor loop — the descending neurons terminate in a nerve cord you do not have.

Why one harness instead of four cloned repos
task observation ──► sensory encoder ──► LIF sim over connectome graph ──► readout ──► action
                     inject current into    the only shared,              descending
                     named populations      expensive part                 neurons
                                                    │
                                                    └──► dopaminergic reward

All four demos are that same three-stage pipeline with different ends. Cloning four repos gets you four incompatible LIF simulators and no way to ask “does the same fly behave consistently across tasks?” — which is the actually interesting question. So each demo here is ~80 lines: which neurons to drive, and which to read.

Two bugs this found by being run rather than read. External current was being accumulated into the decaying synaptic pool every step, building to ~50× its nominal value and pinning every driven population at the refractory limit of 454 Hz. And the pathway weights were 8× too strong, putting downstream populations above 300 Hz — which no fly neuron does. Both are fixed; the rates shown are from after.
