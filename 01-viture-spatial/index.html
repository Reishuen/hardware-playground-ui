VITURE Spatial Brief

Four hand-gesture apps, and the research that decides which glasses to buy and what order to build in. This is a design brief, not a prototype — nothing here has run on hardware yet, because the cheap tests come first.

Hardware verdict
Luma Ultra + Pro Neckband. Not Luma Pro.

The Unity XR SDK runs only on the Pro Neckband — the neckband is the compute unit, so no neckband means no SDK means no app. 3DoF head tracking is on the whole lineup; 6DoF requires Luma Ultra, the only model with the RGB plus dual grayscale depth cameras. All four apps need 6DoF, so Luma Pro plus a neckband would leave you with none of them. Under 3DoF, world-locked objects swim with your head as you translate — the most immersion-killing failure mode in XR, and unfixable in software.

But don't buy yet. Every app's core risk is testable for free in a browser first. The cheapest high-value test is the orchestra's per-seat binaural mix, which needs no glasses and no hand tracking, and it gates the most expensive app.

Instrument families are three different problems

Not variations — three genuinely different control problems, ranked by how well hand tracking can serve them. This is the analysis that decides what the orchestra app actually ships.

One gesture grammar, six primitives

Six is the budget. Every additional gesture costs recognition reliability, because the tracker confuses neighbouring poses, and costs the user memory. If an app needs a seventh interaction, it becomes a mode, not a gesture — which is exactly what playing an instrument is.

The five sims, running in your browser

Built before the hardware, on purpose: every question in this brief except two — latency on the glasses, and whether blowing into a neckband mic feels usable — is answerable on a laptop. Mouse and touch always work; each sim also has real skeletal hand tracking through the webcam, using the same six-gesture grammar the Unity build will use.

Why the violin is not buildable, and the cello is

Semitone spacing is L·2−n/12·(1−2−1/12): a violin's first-position semitone is 18.2 mm and by the 12th it is 9.1 mm — inside the tracking noise band. The cello starts at 38.7 mm and is still 19.4 mm an octave up. So: pitch-snap the fingerboard, or make it a cello. The orchestra prototype does the former.

Three rules that decide whether this feels good at all. Every continuous gesture needs a visible proxy rendered at the pinch, not a cursor elsewhere — with no haptics, visual feedback is the only channel, and this one detail is most of the difference between magic and broken. Discrete gestures need a ~250 ms commit animation with a cancel window, or a stray fist destroys work. And the latency budget is 50 ms, except percussion at 20 ms — measure it on day one, it is the single number that decides which apps survive.
